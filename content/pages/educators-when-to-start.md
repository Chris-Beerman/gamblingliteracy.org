## What the evidence says about timing

Here is the honest answer: **research has not established a single best age or grade for gambling education.** It helps to be clear about what is known, what is a reasonable inference, and what is still open.

::: research "Research finding: no optimal age has been tested"
No trial we found has compared the same gambling program delivered at different ages. A 2023 review of 32 prevention studies recommends starting universal programs "as early as possible," but that is the authors' judgment rather than a tested finding, and they note the field has yet to agree on best practices.[^monreal2023] Most evaluated programs have been delivered in high schools.
:::

::: research "Research finding: the evidence on early starts is mixed"
Some studies find that people who started gambling young, or who gambled regularly as teenagers, are more likely to have gambling problems later. In a UK birth cohort, gambling patterns at 24 often traced back to gambling at 17, and the authors concluded that patterns of problem and moderate-risk gambling were "set by the age of 20."[^emond2019] A Spanish study found that gambling before the legal age went with a higher chance of later problems.[^perez2024]

But other evidence points the other way. A UK government review had moderate confidence that **age, and the age a young person started gambling, are not risk factors** for later harmful gambling,[^ohid2023] and a US study of young adults found no link between onset age and severity.[^quinn2022] Early gambling may be a marker of other risks rather than a cause in itself.
:::

::: practical "Practical suggestion: reach young people before the first legal bet, and keep going after"
Since no age is proven, a sensible approach is to **meet young people at the points where gambling actually enters their lives**, starting before they can legally bet and continuing through the transition to adulthood. That means short, repeated conversations matched to what they're encountering, rather than a single lesson in one grade.
:::

## Stages and what young people meet

The ages below are rough. Young people differ, and so do families and communities.

{{ figure social-influence | Friends watch a game on TV; one holds a phone out toward the others. }}

### Late elementary and middle school (about 10–14)

**What they meet:** loot boxes, card packs and spin-to-win features in games; free-to-play "social casino" apps; scratch tickets as gifts; betting ads during games they watch with family.

**What they can learn:** this is when probability enters the math curriculum, and when young people can start to grasp that chance has no memory. It's a good stage for the [dice activity](/educators/conversation-guide/#activity) and for asking who decides the odds in a game.

### Early high school (about 14–16)

**What they meet:** sports fandom that comes wrapped in odds, parlays and "boosts"; friendly bets and pools with friends; fantasy sports; influencers and streamers who gamble on camera; friends who claim to win.

**What they can learn:** how advertising works and who it's aimed at; the difference between knowing sports and beating a price; what chasing losses looks like.

### Late high school (about 16–18)

**What they meet:** their own money from jobs; more unsupervised time online; peers who bet through older friends, borrowed accounts or unlicensed offshore sites; in many states, the legal lottery at 18.

**What they can learn:** how sign-up offers and "risk-free" bets work; how to recognize when gambling is causing problems for themselves or a friend; where help is.

## Transitions worth planning for

Some moments bring a change in access, money or independence all at once. They are natural points for a check-in.

::: research "Research finding: young adults bet at high rates"
- In a 2023 NCAA survey of 3,527 people aged 18–22, **58%** had done at least one sports betting activity, and **6%** of respondents said they had lost more than $500 on sports betting in a single day.[^ncaa2023] (An online panel survey, released by the NCAA, not peer-reviewed.)
- In Illinois, adults aged 18–24 had the highest rate of past-month sports betting of any age group, at **13.2%**, in a 2021 statewide gambling assessment.[^il2021]
:::

- **The 18th birthday and graduation.** In Illinois, the lottery is legal at 18.[^il-lottery] Graduation often brings money gifts and new independence.
- **Starting college, a job or the military.** Leaving home means less supervision, new peer groups and, for many, the first experience of managing money alone.
- **The 21st birthday.** In Illinois, sports betting, casinos and video gaming terminals are legal at 21.[^il-sports][^il-casino][^il-vgt] Expect betting offers and advertising to become part of their feed.

Minimum ages differ by product and by state. Check the rules where you are.

## Everyday moments

You don't have to wait for a lesson. Most good conversations start with something already happening.

## Questions that remain open

::: open
- **Is there an age at which gambling education works best?** No study has tested this directly.
- **Does starting earlier cause later problems, or mark other risks?** The evidence is mixed (see above).
- **How should education keep up with new products?** Prediction markets, sweepstakes casinos and in-game gambling features change faster than curricula and research.
- **Do short, repeated conversations work better than single lessons?** It is a reasonable idea from other areas of health education, but it hasn't been tested for gambling.
:::

These are among the questions Gambling Literacy hopes to help answer. See the [research agenda](/research-and-advocacy/#research-questions).
