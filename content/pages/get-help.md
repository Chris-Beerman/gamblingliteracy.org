## In an emergency or a crisis

If someone is in immediate danger, **call 911**.

<div class="helpline" markdown="1">
[988](tel:988)
{: .num}

**988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.** Call or text 988, or chat at [chat.988lifeline.org](https://chat.988lifeline.org/). Free, confidential, 24/7. For anyone in emotional distress or thinking about suicide, including young people, and for adults worried about someone.[^988]
</div>

Gambling problems can come with serious distress, debt and shame. If a young person talks about harming themselves, treat that as the priority, whatever the gambling involves.

## Gambling helplines

These lines are free and confidential. Young people, parents, educators and coaches can all call; you don't need to be the person gambling.

<div class="helpline" markdown="1">
[1-800-MY-RESET](tel:+18006973738)
{: .num}

**National Problem Gambling Helpline** (National Council on Problem Gambling). Call or text 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738), or [chat online](https://www.ncpgambling.org/help-treatment/chat/). 24/7, every day of the year. The older number, 1-800-522-4700, still works.[^ncpg]
</div>

<div class="helpline" markdown="1">
[1-800-GAMBLER](tel:+18004262537)
{: .num}

**Illinois Gambling Helpline**, funded by the Illinois Department of Human Services. Call 1-800-GAMBLER (1-800-426-2537), 24 hours a day, free and confidential. More at [AreYouReallyWinning.com](https://e.helplineil.org/areyoureallywinning/).[^il]
</div>

Illinois is listed because it is where Gambling Literacy's editor practices. Outside Illinois, the national helpline can connect you with services in your state, and the National Council on Problem Gambling lists [help by state](https://www.ncpgambling.org/help-treatment/help-by-state/).

## Support made for teenagers

<div class="helpline" markdown="1">
[Teen Line](tel:+18008528336)
{: .num}

**Teen Line** (Didi Hirsch Mental Health Services). Teens talking with trained teen volunteers, supervised by adult professionals, for young people up to age 19. Call (800) 852-8336 from 6 to 10 p.m. Pacific (8 p.m. to midnight Central), or text TEEN to 839863 from 6 to 9 p.m. Pacific. Calls outside those hours go to Didi Hirsch's 24/7 counselors.[^teenline]
</div>

Some young people find it easier to start with a peer. Teen Line isn't a gambling service, but it's a good first step for a teenager who is stressed, ashamed or in trouble and not ready to tell an adult.

## At school

- **The school counselor, social worker or psychologist** is usually the right first step for a student whose gambling worries you. They know the student, the family and the local services, and they follow your school's procedures on confidentiality and parent contact.
- **The school nurse or the student's doctor** can help when gambling sits alongside other health concerns, such as substance use, sleep or mood.
- **Coaches and athletic directors** should know their league's or association's rules on betting by student athletes, and who to report concerns to.

::: practical "If you're an educator worried about a student"
1. Talk with the student first, calmly and privately. The [conversation guide](/educators/conversation-guide/#if-a-young-person-says-they-already-gamble) has suggestions.
2. Bring in your school counselor or student support team, following your school's procedures.
3. Share the helpline numbers above with the student, and with parents or caregivers where appropriate.
4. Don't keep notes or records about a student beyond what your school's procedures require, and never share a student's details outside them.
:::

## For parents and caregivers

The [For Parents](/parents/) page covers how to talk with your child and what to do if they already bet. For help:

- **Call the helpline yourself.** 1-800-MY-RESET and, in Illinois, 1-800-GAMBLER both help family members, including parents of teenagers.
- **Use the controls you already have.** On an iPhone or iPad, [Screen Time and Ask to Buy](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105121) let you approve purchases and limit apps. On Android, [Google Family Link](https://families.google/familylink/) does the same. Xbox, PlayStation and Nintendo consoles have family settings that can block in-game purchases, and your bank can stop or alert on gambling transactions on a card your teenager uses. [BetBlocker](https://betblocker.org/) is free software that blocks gambling sites and apps on phones and computers and has a parental-controls option. None of this stops a determined teenager, but it removes easy access.
- **Look after yourself.** [Gam-Anon](https://www.gam-anon.org/) runs support groups for people affected by someone else's gambling. Its hotline is **844-475-4213**.[^gamanon]
- **Get professional help for yourself if you need it.** If you are an adult in Illinois or Minnesota who wants therapy for your own gambling, or support as a parent, Chris Beerman's practice, [Chicago Addiction Therapy](https://www.chicagoaddictiontherapy.com/), works with adults and families. **It does not treat minors.** For a young person, start with your school counselor, your child's doctor or the helplines above.

## If there is money owed or a threat

Young people who gamble sometimes end up owing money to friends, older teens or someone running bets. If a young person owes money to someone who is pressuring or threatening them, treat it as a **safety issue first**: involve the school's safety or administrative staff, and the police if anyone is in danger. Don't try to settle the debt quietly before you know who it is owed to.

## For young adults (18 and over)

If an older teenager or young adult wants to stop, two tools are worth knowing about:

- **Self-exclusion.** Adults can ask to be banned from gambling with licensed operators. In Illinois, the Illinois Gaming Board runs a self-exclusion program for casinos and sports betting, and the Illinois Lottery runs its own; other states have their own programs. The national helpline can explain how it works where you live.
- **Blocking software.** Free tools such as [BetBlocker](https://betblocker.org/) block gambling sites and apps on a person's own devices.

## What this site can't do

Gambling Literacy is an education site. It can't give individual advice, assess a young person, or respond in an emergency. Please don't send personal or identifying information about a young person by email.
