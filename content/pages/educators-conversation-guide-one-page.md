## Open with what they see

- "Betting ads are everywhere during games. What are they trying to make you feel?"
- "Do you know your odds of getting the item you want from a loot box? Who decides them?"
- "Why do athletic associations have strict rules about players and betting?"
- "That ad said 'risk-free bet.' What do you think that actually means?"

In a group, ask what people their age do, not what each person does. Save personal questions for one-on-one.

## Four ideas worth explaining

1. **The odds favor the house.** Every bet is priced so the operator keeps a share. Bettors as a group lose over time.
2. **Chance has no memory.** After five heads, the next flip is still 50/50. Nothing is "due."
3. **"Free" and "boosted" are marketing.** Bonuses come with conditions designed to keep people betting.
4. **Knowing sports isn't beating the odds.** Prices already reflect what experts know, and near-misses feel closer than they are.

## If they already gamble

- **Stay calm and thank them.** "I'm glad you told me."
- **Get curious.** What, with whom, whose money, how they feel after.
- **Skip the lecture.** Shame ends the conversation.
- **Watch money and mood.** Borrowing, debts, hiding losses or low mood mean it's time for more help.
- **If family is involved,** don't criticize. Ask who places the bets and how often.
- **Know your role.** Listen, stay connected, refer. Follow your school's procedures.

## Get help

<p class="onepage-line"><strong>988</strong> Call or text in a crisis, 24/7</p>
<p class="onepage-line"><strong>1-800-MY-RESET</strong> National Problem Gambling Helpline, call or text, 24/7, free and confidential. Anyone can call, including parents and young people.</p>
<p class="onepage-line"><strong>At school</strong> The counselor, social worker or psychologist</p>
