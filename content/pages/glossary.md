Laws and products change quickly, and rules differ by state. These definitions describe how the terms are generally used in the US; they are not legal advice.

<div class="glossary" markdown="1">

## Betting terms

Odds
:   The price of a bet, which also tells you how likely the operator thinks something is. In US odds, **+500** means a $100 bet wins $500 (an underdog); **−200** means you must bet $200 to win $100 (a favorite).

House edge, or "the vig"
:   The share the operator builds into the price of every bet, so that bettors as a group lose over time. Also called the juice or the hold.

Parlay
:   One bet that combines several picks. It pays more, but only if every pick wins. Each pick is called a **leg**. "I was one leg away" is a common near-miss.

Prop bet
:   A bet on something within a game rather than the final result, such as how many points one player scores. Props on individual college athletes are restricted in some states.

Live betting, or in-game betting
:   Betting while a game is being played, with odds that change by the second.

Odds boost
:   A promotion that raises the payout on a particular bet, often a parlay. The boosted bet is usually still priced in the operator's favor.

Bonus bets, or "free bets"
:   Credit from a sportsbook, usually for signing up or depositing. It is not cash: it usually expires, often requires a deposit first, and a winning bonus bet typically pays only the winnings, not the amount of the bet. The [mock-ad worksheet](/educators/conversation-guide/mock-ad/) walks through an example.

Sportsbook
:   A company that takes bets on sports, usually through an app. Licensed sportsbooks operate in states that have legalized sports betting, most with a minimum age of 21.

Offshore site
:   A betting or casino website based outside the US and not licensed by any state. Offshore sites may not check age, and there is little protection if they don't pay out.

## Games and apps

Loot box
:   A paid, random reward in a video game: you pay, real money or in-game currency, without knowing what you'll get. Card packs and spin-to-win wheels work the same way.

Social casino
:   A free-to-play app with casino games such as slots or poker. You can't win money, but you can buy more chips with real money.

Sweepstakes casino
:   An online casino-style site that uses two kinds of virtual coins, one of which can be cashed out for prizes or money, and claims to be a sweepstakes rather than gambling. Several states have moved to ban them.

Daily fantasy sports, and pick'em apps
:   Paid contests in which players pick athletes or predict their stats and win money if they do well. Some states treat them as legal contests with an age limit of 18 or 21; others treat them as gambling.

Prediction market
:   A site or app where people buy and sell contracts that pay out if something happens, such as which team wins or who wins an election. Supporters call it trading; critics call it betting. Their legal status is being fought over between federal and state regulators, and their age limits can differ from sportsbooks'.

Skin gambling
:   Betting with cosmetic items from video games ("skins") that have real-world value, often on unlicensed sites.

## Ideas about chance

Gambler's fallacy
:   The belief that a win is "due" after a run of losses, as if chance has a memory. It doesn't. The [dice activity](/educators/conversation-guide/dice-tally/) shows why.

Near-miss
:   A result that feels close to a win, like two cherries and a lemon, or a parlay that lost by one leg. Near-misses make people feel they are getting closer when they aren't.

Chasing losses
:   Betting more, or more often, to win back money already lost. It is one of the criteria clinicians use to diagnose gambling disorder.

## Getting help

Self-exclusion
:   A program that lets an adult ban themselves from gambling with licensed operators for a set period. States run their own programs, usually for casinos, sports betting and sometimes the lottery. See [Get help](/get-help/#for-young-adults-18-and-over).

Blocking software
:   Apps that block gambling sites and apps on a phone or computer. Some, such as [BetBlocker](https://betblocker.org/), are free and include parental controls.

Gambling disorder
:   The clinical name for a gambling problem serious enough to meet diagnostic criteria. Only a qualified professional can diagnose it. Nothing on this site is a diagnosis.

## What "gambling literacy" means {#gambling-literacy}

A gambling-literate young person understands four things: **how chance and odds work**, including why the house always comes out ahead over time; **how gambling products and ads are designed** to keep people playing and betting; **what it looks like when gambling starts to cause harm**, in themselves or a friend; and **where to go for help**. This site exists to help adults teach those four things.
