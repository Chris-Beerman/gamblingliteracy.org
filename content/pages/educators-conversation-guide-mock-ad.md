## Read the ad

1. What is the biggest number in the ad? What do they want you to feel when you see it?<br><span class="blank full"></span>
2. Find the asterisk (\*). What do you have to do **before** you get anything?<br><span class="blank full"></span>
3. Is "$200 in Bonus Bets" the same as $200 in cash? Find the sentence in the fine print that tells you.<br><span class="blank full"></span>
4. What happens to the bonus bets if you don't use them within a week? Why might the company set a deadline?<br><span class="blank full"></span>
5. A parlay is one bet that combines several picks and pays only if every pick wins. Why would a company "boost" parlays in particular?<br><span class="blank full"></span>

::: practical "For the teacher"
SampleBet and this ad are made up. The terms are a composite of conditions common in US sportsbook welcome offers; real terms vary by company and state. Points to draw out:

- **The deposit and the first bet come first.** Nothing is free until the customer has put in their own money.
- **Bonus bets are not cash.** When a bonus bet wins, the customer usually gets only the winnings, not the bet amount back, so $200 in bonus bets is worth much less than $200.
- **Deadlines push people to bet quickly** rather than decide calmly.
- **Parlays are harder to win than single bets,** because every pick must win, and the house edge on each leg adds up. Boosting them steers customers toward bets that favor the company even more.
- **Who is the ad for?** Bright colors, big numbers and "claim now" urgency are aimed at people who act fast. The age limit is in the smallest type on the page.

Use this sheet instead of showing students a real betting ad or app. This is a practical classroom activity, not a tested intervention.
:::
