## Who this is for

Middle- and high-school teachers (especially in health, math, PE and advisory), school counselors, social workers and psychologists, coaches and athletic directors, youth workers, and the parents and carers they work with.

## Two things to keep in mind

::: research
Gambling education programs in schools reliably improve what students know about gambling and correct common misconceptions. Whether they reduce gambling or gambling harm later is much less certain, because few studies have followed students long enough to find out. See [what the evidence says](/research-and-advocacy/#what-programs-change).
:::

::: practical
So treat education as one part of prevention, not the whole of it. What adults do matters too: noticing, staying connected, limiting easy access, questioning the advertising, and knowing where to send someone who needs help.
:::

## What this guide is not

It is not a curriculum, not a screening tool and not clinical advice. It doesn't ask you to label any student as "at risk." Its aim is to help you offer good general education to every young person and recognize when one of them needs more.
